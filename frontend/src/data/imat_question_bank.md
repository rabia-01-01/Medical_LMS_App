# 1. Reading Skills and General Knowledge — 50 Questions

## Reading comprehension and language skills

**Passage 1:** *Vaccination trains the immune system to recognize a pathogen without requiring a person to experience the full disease. A vaccine may contain an inactivated pathogen, a harmless component of it, or instructions that enable cells to produce a target antigen. After vaccination, immune cells can form memory responses. Protection is not always immediate, and its strength can vary with the vaccine and the individual.*

**Q1.** What is the central idea of the passage?
A. Vaccines always prevent every infection.  
B. Vaccines prepare the immune system to recognize a pathogen.  
C. Vaccines work only by containing live pathogens.  
D. Vaccines remove all pathogens from the environment.  
**Answer: B.** The passage explains how vaccination prepares immune recognition and memory.

A. Made unable to cause the usual infection.

C. Removed from the body.  
D. Able to reproduce rapidly.  
**Answer: A.** An inactivated pathogen has been rendered unable to cause the usual disease.

A. Every vaccine contains a complete pathogen.  
B. Protection begins immediately in all people.  
C. Some vaccines provide cells with instructions to make an antigen.  
D. Immune memory is unrelated to vaccination.  
**Answer: C.** The passage explicitly describes vaccine instructions that lead cells to produce a target antigen.

**Q4.** The phrase “its strength” refers to the strength of:
A. The pathogen.  
B. The immune response or protection.  
C. The vaccine packaging.  
D. The infection period.  
**Answer: B.** The nearest relevant idea is the protection generated after vaccination.

**Answer: A.** The passage states that immune cells can form memory responses.

# 2. Logical Reasoning and Problems — 50 Questions

## Deduction, arguments and conditions

**Q1.** All surgeons are doctors. Some doctors are researchers. Which conclusion must be true?
A. All surgeons are researchers.  
B. Some researchers are surgeons.  
C. All surgeons are doctors.  
D. No researchers are doctors.  
**Answer: C.** This simply restates the universal premise; the other options do not necessarily follow.

**Q2.** If every A is B and no B is C, which statement must be true?
A. Some A are C.  
B. No A is C.  
C. Every C is A.  
D. Some B are not A.  
**Answer: B.** Since A is wholly inside B and B is disjoint from C, A cannot overlap C.

**Q3.** “If the alarm rings, the staff evacuate.” The alarm rings. What follows logically?
A. The staff evacuate.  
B. The alarm did not ring.  
C. The staff remain inside.  
D. No conclusion is possible.  
**Answer: A.** This is modus ponens: P implies Q; P is true; therefore Q.

**Q4.** “If the sample is contaminated, the test is repeated.” The test was not repeated. Assuming the rule is always followed, what follows?
A. The sample was contaminated.  
B. The sample was not contaminated.  
C. The sample was diluted.  
D. The test was positive.  
**Answer: B.** By modus tollens, not Q implies not P.

**Q5.** Which argument is logically valid?
A. All mammals breathe; whales are mammals; therefore whales breathe.  
B. All birds fly; penguins are birds; therefore penguins fly.  
C. Some students study; therefore all students study.  
D. If it rains, roads are wet; roads are wet; therefore it rained.  
**Answer: A.** The conclusion follows from the two premises. The others contain false premises or affirm the consequent / overgeneralise.

**Q6.** If P is true and Q is false, what is the truth value of P AND Q?
A. True.  
B. False.  
C. Cannot be determined.  
D. Both true and false.  
**Answer: B.** A conjunction is true only when both components are true.

**Q7.** If P is false and Q is false, what is the truth value of P OR Q (inclusive OR)?
A. True.  
B. False.  
C. Cannot be determined.  
D. True only if P is true.  
**Answer: B.** Inclusive OR is false only when both statements are false.

**Q8.** The negation of “All applicants passed” is:
A. No applicant passed.  
B. At least one applicant did not pass.  
C. All applicants failed.  
D. At least one applicant passed.  
**Answer: B.** Negating “all” gives “at least one does not”.

**Q9.** “A student can enter the laboratory only if wearing a coat.” Which condition is necessary for entry?
A. Wearing a coat.  
B. Carrying a calculator.  
C. Being a researcher.  
D. Wearing gloves only.  
**Answer: A.** “Only if” identifies a necessary condition.

**Q10.** If the statement “Some A are B” is true, which statement must also be true?
A. Some B are A.  
B. All A are B.  
C. No A are B.  
D. All B are A.  
**Answer: A.** The overlap relation is symmetric.

## Quantitative and relational reasoning

**Q21.** A clinic sees 24 patients in 3 hours at a constant rate. How many patients does it see in 5 hours?
A. 30.  
B. 36.  
C. 40.  
D. 45.  
**Answer: C.** Rate = 24/3 = 8 patients per hour; 8×5 = 40.

**Q22.** A solution contains 2 parts solute and 8 parts solvent. What fraction of the total mixture is solute?
A. 1/5.  
B. 1/4.  
C. 1/2.  
D. 4/5.  
**Answer: A.** Total parts = 10; solute fraction = 2/10 = 1/5.

**Q23.** A test has 40 questions. A candidate answers 32 correctly. What percentage is correct?
A. 70%.  
B. 75%.  
C. 80%.  
D. 85%.  
**Answer: C.** (32/40)×100 = 80%.

**Q24.** The average of 4, 8, 10 and x is 9. Find x.
A. 12.  
B. 13.  
C. 14.  
D. 16.  
**Answer: C.** Total must be 4×9 = 36; x = 36−22 = 14.

**Q25.** A value rises from 80 to 100. What is the percentage increase?
A. 20%.  
B. 25%.  
C. 30%.  
D. 40%.  
**Answer: B.** Increase = 20; percentage increase = 20/80×100 = 25%.

**Q26.** Five machines make 100 items in 4 hours. At the same rate, how many items do 10 machines make in 4 hours?
A. 100.  
B. 150.  
C. 200.  
D. 400.  
**Answer: C.** Doubling the machines doubles output for the same time.

**Q27.** A train travels 150 km in 2.5 hours. Its average speed is:
A. 50 km/h.  
B. 55 km/h.  
C. 60 km/h.  
D. 75 km/h.

**Q28.** If 3x − 5 = 16, x equals:
A. 5.

A. 1/5.

**Q30.** Two fair coins are tossed. What is the probability of obtaining exactly one head?

**Q31.** If A is taller than B and B is taller than C, who is shortest?
A. A.  
B. B.  
C. C.

A. Ana.  
B. Bo.

A. B-A-C.  
B. A-B-C.  
C. C-B-A.

**Q34.** In a queue, Mira is 7th from the front and 9th from the back. How many people are in the queue?
A. 14.  
B. 15.  
C. 16.  
D. 17.

**Q35.** If today is Wednesday, what day will it be 17 days later?
A. Friday.  
B. Saturday.  
C. Sunday.  
D. Monday.  
**Answer: B.** 17 mod 7 = 3; three days after Wednesday is Saturday.

## Problem solving and data interpretation

**Q36.** A study reports that 60 of 100 participants improved. What is the observed improvement proportion?
A. 0.06.  
B. 0.4.

**Q37.** A graph shows values 10, 12, 12, 16 and 20. What is the median?
A. 12.  
B. 14.  
C. 16.  
D. 20.  
**Answer: A.** The middle value in the ordered five observations is 12.

**Q38.** The mean of 2, 3, 5 and 10 is:
A. 4.  
B. 5.  
C. 6.  
D. 7.  
**Answer: B.** Sum = 20; mean = 20/4 = 5.

**Q39.** A medicine dose is 5 mg per kg. What dose is required for a 60 kg patient under this stated rule?
A. 12 mg.  
B. 60 mg.  
C. 300 mg.  
D. 600 mg.  
**Answer: C.** 5×60 = 300 mg. This is an arithmetic exercise, not clinical dosing advice.

**Q40.** A map uses a scale of 1 cm : 5 km. A road measures 7 cm on the map. Its represented length is:
A. 12 km.  
B. 25 km.  
C. 35 km.  
D. 70 km.  
**Answer: C.** 7×5 = 35 km.

**Q41.** A box has 4 white, 3 black and 3 green counters. What is the probability of not choosing black?
A. 3/10.  
B. 4/10.  
C. 7/10.  
D. 9/10.  
**Answer: C.** Seven of the ten counters are not black.

**Q42.** If a number is divisible by both 2 and 3, it must be divisible by:
A. 5.  
B. 6.  
C. 8.  
D. 9.  
**Answer: B.** A number divisible by 2 and 3 is divisible by their least common multiple, 6.

**Q43.** Which is the best evidence that a new teaching method caused improved scores?

D. The method is popular online.

**Q44.** A conclusion is “strongly supported” when:
A. It is consistent with the available reliable evidence.

**Q45.** Which statement is an opinion rather than a directly verifiable fact?

**Q46.** A report gives a correlation between screen time and sleep duration. What can be concluded from correlation alone?

**Q47.** A survey includes only volunteers from a fitness club. The main concern is:

**Q48.** If an argument attacks the speaker instead of addressing the evidence, it commits:

**Answer: A.** An ad hominem argument targets the person rather than the claim.

**Q49.** “This treatment worked for one person, so it works for everyone” is an example of:
A. Hasty generalisation.

C. A valid universal law.

When antibiotics are used unnecessarily, bacteria that can survive exposure may become more common. This process contributes to antimicrobial resistance. Public-health specialists therefore recommend using antibiotics only when clinically appropriate and following a qualified professional's instructions.*

**Q5. What is the thesis of the passage?**

A. All infections should be treated with antibiotics.  
B. Antibiotics are ineffective against every microorganism.  
C. Responsible antibiotic use helps limit antimicrobial resistance.  
D. Viral infections always develop into bacterial infections.

**Answer: C.**  
**Explanation:** The passage explains why unnecessary antibiotic use contributes to resistance and recommends appropriate use.

**Q6. Which inference is most reasonable?**

A. Taking antibiotics for a cold is always beneficial.  
B. Antibiotics should be selected without medical advice.  
C. Misuse of antibiotics can reduce their future effectiveness.  
D. Bacteria cannot adapt to environmental pressures.

**Answer: C.**  
**Explanation:** If resistant bacteria become more common, treatments that once worked may become less effective.

**Q7. The word “susceptible” in the passage means:**

A. Resistant to treatment.  
B. Capable of being affected by something.  
C. Completely harmless.  
D. Impossible to detect.

**Answer: B.**  
**Explanation:** Susceptible bacteria are bacteria that can be affected by the antibiotic.

**Q8. Which statement is NOT supported by the passage?**

A. Antibiotics can act against susceptible bacteria.  
B. Antibiotics do not treat viral infections such as the common cold.  
C. Unnecessary use can contribute to antimicrobial resistance.  
D. Every bacterial infection requires the same antibiotic.

**Answer: D.**  
**Explanation:** The passage does not say that all bacterial infections require identical treatment.

### Topic 1.2: Textual Cohesion, Coherence & Rhetorical Devices

**Q9. Choose the best connector: “The sample size was small; _____, the findings should be interpreted cautiously.”**

A. therefore  
B. nevertheless  
C. for example  
D. similarly

**Answer: A.**  
**Explanation:** “Therefore” introduces a consequence: a small sample size leads to cautious interpretation.

**Q10. Which sentence best completes the paragraph?**

*The heart pumps blood through a network of vessels. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, while veins return blood to it. _____*

A. The digestive system breaks down food in the stomach.  
B. Capillaries allow the exchange of substances between blood and tissues.  
C. Bones provide structural support to the body.  
D. Neurons transmit electrical signals.

**Answer: B.**  
**Explanation:** The sentence continues the paragraph's explanation of blood vessels.

**Q11. Identify the rhetorical device in: “The laboratory was a beehive of activity.”**

A. Simile  
B. Metaphor  
C. Irony  
D. Alliteration

**Answer: B.**  
**Explanation:** The laboratory is directly described as a beehive to suggest intense activity; no “like” or “as” is used.

**Q12. Which option creates the clearest logical sequence?**

1. The blood sample was collected.  
2. It was labelled with the participant's code.  
3. The sample was analysed.  
4. The results were recorded.

A. 3–1–4–2  
B. 1–2–3–4  
C. 2–4–1–3  
D. 4–3–2–1

**Answer: B.**  
**Explanation:** Collection must precede labelling, analysis and recording of results.

**Q13. Choose the best transition: “The treatment reduced fever in most participants. _____, it did not significantly change the duration of the illness.”**

A. Furthermore  
B. In contrast  
C. For instance  
D. Consequently

**Answer: B.**  
**Explanation:** “In contrast” signals a difference between the improvement in fever and the lack of change in illness duration.

**Q14. Which sentence contains an unclear pronoun reference?**

A. The nurse checked the patient's temperature.  
B. After the doctor spoke to the nurse, she updated the chart.  
C. The laboratory reported the result on Monday.  
D. The students reviewed the chapter together.

**Answer: B.**  
**Explanation:** “She” could refer to either the doctor or the nurse, so the reference is ambiguous.

**Q15. What is the primary purpose of a rhetorical question in a scientific article?**

A. Always to request a numerical answer.  
B. To engage readers or emphasise an issue.  
C. To replace all supporting evidence.  
D. To introduce an unrelated topic.

**Answer: B.**  
**Explanation:** A rhetorical question is generally used for emphasis or engagement rather than to obtain a literal answer.

## Chapter 3: Applied Grammar, Sentence Structure & Clinical Clarity (17 Questions)

### Topic 3.1: Advanced Syntax: Subject-Verb Agreement, Tenses & Conditionals

**Q34. Choose the grammatically correct sentence.**

A. The list of symptoms are long.  
B. The list of symptoms is long.  
C. The list of symptoms were long.  
D. The list of symptoms have long.

**Answer: B.**  
**Explanation:** The subject is “list,” which is singular; “of symptoms” does not change the subject.

**Q35. Choose the correct verb: “Neither the doctor nor the nurses _____ available.”**

A. is  
B. was  
C. are  
D. has

**Answer: C.**  
**Explanation:** With “neither…nor,” the verb generally agrees with the nearer subject; “nurses” is plural.

**Q36. Complete the sentence: “By the time the ambulance arrived, the patient _____ consciousness.”**

A. loses  
B. had lost  
C. has lose  
D. will lose

**Answer: B.**  
**Explanation:** The past perfect describes an event completed before another past event.

**Q37. Choose the correct conditional: “If water reaches 100°C at standard atmospheric pressure, it _____.”**

A. boiled  
B. will boiled  
C. boils  
D. would boiling

**Answer: C.**  
**Explanation:** The zero conditional uses the present simple for general scientific facts.

**Q38. Choose the correct sentence.**

A. Each of the samples have been labelled.  
B. Each of the samples has been labelled.  
C. Each of the samples are labelled.  
D. Each of the samples were labelled.

**Answer: B.**  
**Explanation:** “Each” is singular and takes “has.”

**Q39. Complete the sentence: “The researchers _____ the data for three months before publishing the report.”**

A. analyse  
B. have analysed  
C. had been analysing  
D. will analyse

**Answer: C.**  
**Explanation:** The past perfect continuous describes an activity continuing up to a point in the past.
